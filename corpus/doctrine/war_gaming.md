# War Gaming (MDMP Step 4)

War gaming is **COA Analysis** — Step 4 of MDMP. It is a disciplined visualization of each course of action through critical events, not a head-to-head comparison of COAs.

## What the staff does

1. List friendly forces, assumptions, critical events, and decision points
2. Choose a war-gaming method
3. Record results in a **synchronization matrix** using **action → reaction → counteraction**
4. Note strengths and weaknesses **per COA** (advantages, disadvantages, synchronization risk, likely enemy reactions)
5. Refine COAs and evaluation criteria based on findings
6. Translate qualitative observations into inputs for the Step 5 decision matrix (when numeric scoring is used)

## War-gaming methods

| Method | When to use |
|--------|-------------|
| **Belt** | Fight the battlefield in sequential belts (often east–west or along phases) |
| **Avenue-in-depth** | Follow a single avenue of approach through critical events |
| **Box** | Focus on a defined area (objective, engagement area, choke point) |

## Rules of thumb

- Fight one COA at a time (or one friendly COA against one enemy COA in a structured sequence)
- Do **not** rank COAs during the war game — ranking belongs to Step 5
- Capture decision points where the commander may need to decide or refine the plan
- Update assumptions when the war game reveals invalid premises

## Outputs

Typical products include a synchronization matrix, refined COAs, updated evaluation criteria, and a war-game workbook or narrative that feeds the COA comparison brief.
