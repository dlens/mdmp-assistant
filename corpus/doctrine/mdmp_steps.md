# MDMP — Seven Steps

The **Military Decision-Making Process (MDMP)** is the Army's iterative staff planning methodology (FM 5-0 / ADP 5-0). The commander and staff repeat the process as the situation evolves.

## The seven steps

| Step | Name | Purpose |
|------|------|---------|
| 1 | Receipt of Mission | Alert the staff; gather tools; issue planning guidance |
| 2 | Mission Analysis | Analyze higher HQ order, develop running estimate, define problem, draft commander's intent, develop initial CCIRs, **develop evaluation criteria** |
| 3 | COA Development | Generate options; screen COAs (feasible, acceptable, suitable, distinguishable, complete) |
| 4 | COA Analysis (War Gaming) | Visualize each COA; identify strengths, weaknesses, refinements; **do not compare COAs head-to-head** |
| 5 | COA Comparison | Compare COAs against evaluation criteria (decision matrix / brief) |
| 6 | COA Approval | Commander selects or modifies a COA |
| 7 | Orders Production | Produce OPORD and supporting products |

## Step 4 vs Step 5 (critical boundary)

- **Step 4 (War Gaming):** Staff fight each COA separately. Record action–reaction–counteraction, synchronization issues, and COA-specific strengths and weaknesses.
- **Step 5 (COA Comparison):** Staff compare all surviving COAs against the criteria developed during mission analysis. This is where a decision matrix or similar tool is used.

Comparing COAs during the war game blurs the process and often produces biased narratives before criteria are applied systematically.

## Commander's role

The commander drives the process, provides planning guidance, approves evaluation criteria, selects the COA at Step 6, and issues intent that guides all steps.
