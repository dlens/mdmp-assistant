# COA Development and Screening (MDMP Step 3)

During **COA Development**, the staff generates multiple courses of action that could accomplish the mission. Before war gaming, COAs are screened to ensure they are worth analyzing.

## FASDC screening criteria

A valid COA should be:

| Criterion | Meaning |
|-----------|---------|
| **Feasible** | Can be accomplished with available resources and time |
| **Acceptable** | Worth the cost in terms of risk, casualties, and mission impact |
| **Suitable** | Accomplishes the mission within the commander's intent |
| **Distinguishable** | Meaningfully different from other COAs (not cosmetic variants) |
| **Complete** | Addresses decisive, shaping, and sustaining operations as required |

COAs that fail screening are revised or dropped before Step 4.

## Evaluation criteria (Step 2 and refinements)

**Evaluation criteria** are developed during **Mission Analysis (Step 2)** and refined after war gaming. They describe how the staff will judge COAs in Step 5.

Good criteria are:

- Linked to the mission and commander's intent
- Measurable or assessable where possible
- Few enough to be manageable (staff typically narrows a long list)
- Understood by the commander before comparison

Step 17 of the MDMP planning process (within mission analysis / COA development) covers developing COA evaluation criteria. Criteria should not be invented for the first time during Step 5.

## Decision matrix (Step 5)

The **decision matrix** plots COAs against evaluation criteria. Cells may use narrative assessment or numeric scores derived from war-game findings. The matrix supports a recommendation; the commander decides at Step 6.
